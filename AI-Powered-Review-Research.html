Step 01

Course Syllabus Framing & Parallel Execution

Pedagogical Starting Point

  • Curriculum Alignment: Every research assignment begins with a clearly defined course progression and learning objective.
  • Identical Task Scope: Students receive a specific research mandate mirroring real-world analytical challenges.

Instructor Benchmark Execution

  • Walking the Talk: I complete the exact same research assignment myself every single time.
  • Quality Calibration: Experiencing source bottlenecks, ambiguities, and tool limits firsthand sets realistic expectations for student deliverables.

Step 02

Interactive Research Question Engineering

  • Conversational Problem Framing: Using the assignment description and syllabus as input, I enter an interactive dialogue with a general-purpose LLM (typically ChatGPT).
  • Iterative Decomposition: Broad thematic areas are systematically broken down into sharp, testable research questions suitable for scientific search engines.
  • Applied Example (Recycling Technologies): Through iterative refinement, the domain was structured into five distinct research questions specifically tailored for querying Consensus.
Key Takeaway: Never paste a vague topic straight into a literature search engine. Use a conversational LLM first to engineer precision queries.
Step 02 — Question Engineering Slide 4 / 13
Step 03

Scientific Querying & Markdown Consolidation

1. Consensus Literature Runs

  • All five engineered research questions are submitted sequentially to Consensus.
  • Generated synthesis reports are skimmed immediately to verify relevance and coverage of core recycling technologies.

2. Master Markdown Assembly

  • All five individual reports are concatenated into one unified master Markdown file.
  • Creates a single, clean, machine-readable corpus ready for downstream auditing and cross-referencing.
Step 03 — Evidence Synthesis Slide 5 / 13
Step 04

Cross-LLM Logical Consistency & Gap Audit

  • Managing Source Complexity: Merging five reports introduces dozens of citations, overlapping claims, and potential contradictions that are hard to spot manually.
  • Second-Opinion Architecture: The consolidated Markdown file is passed to an analytically strong second model — primarily Claude or Gemini — with an explicit audit prompt: "Analyze this entire corpus. Is it logically consistent? Where are the flaws or blind spots?"
  • Preventing Automation Blindness: Relying on a single AI pipeline invites uncritical acceptance; multi-model cross-checking surfaces hidden inconsistencies early.
Step 04 — Cross-LLM Audit Slide 6 / 13
Step 05

Building a Dedicated Gemini Notebook Repository

  • Structured Knowledge Base First: Before creating a single presentation slide or handout, I build a dedicated source repository inside Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM).
  • Grounded Workspace: All validated reports, key papers, and technical references for the specific case are indexed in one isolated project environment.
  • Traceable Retrieval: Every subsequent query, comparison, or slide draft draws directly from verified project documents rather than ungrounded model memory.
Step 05 — Knowledge Repository Slide 7 / 13
Step 06

Continuous Source Expansion & Open-Access Focus

Beyond Consensus Defaults

  • Continuous monitoring throughout the workflow to identify missing perspectives or niche technical details.
  • Targeted searches across multiple LLMs to incorporate specialized primary sources and broaden the evidence base.

Strict No-Paywall Rule

  • Every selected source must be freely downloadable.
  • Students must be able to read, verify, and work with the exact same documents without hitting paywall barriers.
Step 06 — Open-Access Expansion Slide 8 / 13
Step 07

Concrete Session Preparation & Aspect Mapping

  • Transition to Teaching Design: Moving from broad literature collection into concrete preparation for the specific lecture session.
  • Structured Aspect Inventory: Synthesizing the accumulated repository into a clear, comprehensive overview list of all topics to be discussed that day.
  • Applied Example: Systematically mapping the spectrum of recycling technologies (mechanical, chemical, thermal, solvent-based) into a logical didactic sequence.

Step 08

Evidence-Backed Slide Construction

  • Iterative Slide Authoring: Building out the lecture deck (or modular multi-deck series) section by section from the aspect inventory.
  • Real-Time Fact & Claim Verification: Continuously querying Consensus while drafting to clarify technical mechanisms, yield rates, and boundary conditions.
  • Dual-Dimension Rigor: Ensuring that both technical parameters and economic assessments on every slide are anchored in traceable peer-reviewed sources.

Step 09

Position Consolidation & Cross-Cultural Triangulation

  • The 10-Hour Deep-Dive Threshold: After roughly 10 hours of intensive reading, auditing, and structuring, an informed academic viewpoint (Lehrmeinung) naturally crystallizes.
  • Final Multi-LLM Synthesis: Engaging in closing synthesis dialogues across diverse models — including ChatGPT, Gemini, and Qwen (especially for international and Asian market/technology perspectives) — to stress-test consistency and articulate a coherent concluding position.

Epistemic Reflection

Is This "The Truth"? — The Limits of Any Synthesis

High Effort ≠ Absolute Truth

  • We operate inside a fast-moving, complex research frontier where even 10 hours of rigorous AI-assisted review yields only a provisional snapshot.
  • Is Prof. Rabehl's synthesis the definitive truth of how the world works? No — of course not. It is simply the best evidence I could assemble right now.
  • Your Mandate: Question these findings, inspect the open-access sources, and form your own independent scientific judgment.
"The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool. So you have to be very careful about that. After you've not fooled yourself, it's easy not to fool other scientists."

Scientific Integrity: Rigorous self-critique is not a weakness — it is the defining habit of every credible researcher.

Workflow Summary

Core Principles for Your Own Research

1. Triangulate Models

Never trust a single LLM output. Combine conversational framing (ChatGPT), scientific search (Consensus), logical auditing (Claude/Gemini), and international perspectives (Qwen).

2. Ground in Open Sources

Build a verifiable source repository first. Prioritize accessible, downloadable primary literature so every claim can be audited by peers and students.

3. Stay Self-Critical

AI accelerates literature synthesis, not wisdom. Treat every review as a provisional map — verify the territory and form your own reasoned conclusion.

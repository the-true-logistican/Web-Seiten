SLIDE 01 / 16 · INTRODUCTION

The Greenfield Illusion in Plastics Circularity

01 / 16

Higher recycling quotas and polymer depolymerization chemistry fail in practice when they collide with unyielding legacy collection architecture.

Core Lecture Positioning

  • The Academic Fallacy: Textbooks assume zero-friction greenfield environments with pure feedstock streams.
  • The Industrial Reality: Engineers always operate inside legacy brownfield infrastructure with pre-existing contracts, legacy sorting facilities, and contaminated material flows.
  • The Paradigm Shift: Stop designing ideal reactors for non-existent pristine streams. Start engineering for messy, legacy realities.
PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"You have been taught that circular economy is a matter of setting higher recycling quotas and designing better polymers. Today, we dismantle that assumption. We examine why state-of-the-art chemical and mechanical recycling technologies fail in practice—not because the chemistry is flawed, but because they collide with existing brownfield collection infrastructure."

SLIDE 02 / 16 · EPISTEMIC CONSTRAINTS

The Core Premise: Engineering vs. Reality

02 / 16

Greenfield Teaching (The Happy Path)

  • − Assumes zero initial physical friction
  • − Guarantees clean, homogeneous polymer input
  • − Unbounded design freedom and floating site boundaries
  • − Solves deterministic mass-balance equations

Brownfield Reality (The Industrial Field)

  • + Must plug into decades-old municipal networks
  • + Handles stochastically variable material streams
  • + Constrained by long-term incineration concessions
  • + Absorbs legacy chemical additives and PFAs

The Core Engineering Verdict

If your circular economy model requires a greenfield society to function, it is not an engineering solution; it is a laboratory fantasy.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"In university exercises, we give you clean boundary conditions so you can practice solving the math. The danger is when you graduate and think the world looks like those exercises. When you enter industry, there is always an existing hall, an existing waste collection contract, and an established logistics operator who owns the bin."

SLIDE 03 / 16 · SCALING FAILURE

The Plastics Paradox: Lab Ideals vs. Scaled Failure

03 / 16

Despite billions in capital expenditures for advanced chemical recycling pilots and strict European legislative quotas, actual closed-loop plastic recycling rates remain under 10%.

Discrepancy between lab-rated chemical reactor conversion efficiencies and real post-consumer waste yields due to collection contamination.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"Look at chemical recycling pilot plants across Europe. The chemistry works in the lab at 95% efficiency. But when scaled up to a commercial 50,000-ton plant fed by municipal solid waste, yield drops to 30% or the plant shuts down entirely because PVC trace levels poison the catalysts. The problem wasn't the chemical reactor—it was the collection input."

SLIDE 04 / 16 · SYSTEMIC DIAGNOSTIC

The Greenfield/Brownfield Matrix for Circular Economy

04 / 16

Classification of circular engineering challenges across physical infrastructure and material feedstock variance:

CELL 1: GREENFIELD PILOT (LAB)

New Site + Pure Feedstock

Academic textbook problems. Pyrolysis of pure industrial offcuts (PIR) in custom facility.

CELL 2: INDUSTRIAL GREENFIELD

New Site + Uncertain Feedstock

Commercial plant built for municipal waste. Fails due to unexpected input contamination.

CELL 3: BROWNFIELD RETROFIT

Existing Site + Pure Feedstock

Retrofitting clean industrial offcut collection into established plant floor space.

CELL 4: REAL-WORLD LEGACY REALITY

Existing Site + Mixed Legacy Feedstock

Where Circular Economy Actually Happens: Integrating multi-layer consumer packaging into entrenched municipal sorting rules and long-term contracts.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"Students always design for Cell 1. But real engineering practice takes place in Cell 4. Notice that Cell 4 forces you to deal with physical constraints (the existing sorting hall) AND material constraints (the dirty yellow bin)."

SLIDE 05 / 16 · SYSTEMIC FOCUS

Chapter Focus: The Collection Bottleneck

05 / 16

Collection is not a trivial transport vector. It is an entrenched socio-technical regime optimized for linear mass disposal, not material purity.

  • Municipal waste systems were engineered to maximize throughput per vehicle-hour, not polymer purity.
  • Waste collection trucks compact mixed garbage, crushing glass into plastic pores and embedding organic sludge.
PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"We are in the 'Collection' chapter of this course. Students often assume collection is just logistics: trucks picking up bins. But collection is where the material identity is corrupted. Compacting mixed waste in a rear-loader truck creates a physical bond between contaminants and polymers that no washing line can undo."

SLIDE 06 / 16 · INFRASTRUCTURE INERTIA

Anatomy of Legacy Collection Systems

06 / 16
  1. 1

    Material Recovery Facility (MRF) Mechanical Limits

    Sorting plants built 20 years ago use optical near-infrared (NIR) scanners set for rigid PET/HDPE bottles. Flexible films wrap around rotating trommel screens, causing daily operational shutdowns.

  2. 2

    Contamination by Compression

    Collection compaction hydraulic rams embed paper fibers and trace PVC into soft polyolefins, increasing cross-contamination before sorting even begins.

  3. 3

    Contractual and Financial Lock-In

    Municipalities operate under 15-to-25-year Waste-to-Energy (incineration) contracts with strict minimum-tonnage guarantees (pay-or-play penalties), financially penalizing material diversion.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"Even if you design a perfect recycling method, you cannot simply ask a municipality to change its sorting trucks or cancel a 20-year incineration contract. The city will face multi-million euro penalties. Your engineering solution must work within these legal and contractual walls."

SLIDE 07 / 16 · MATERIAL FRICTION

The Multi-Layer Problem: Trapped Polymers

07 / 16

Modern food packaging uses multi-layer films (e.g., PET / EVOH / PE) to extend shelf life. Legacy optical collection sorting treats these as homogenous waste.

Packaging Layer Material Function Recycling Failure Mode in Legacy Streams
Outer Layer (PET) Printability & Structural Rigidity Optical scanner reads PET, routes whole film to rigid stream.
Middle Layer (EVOH) Oxygen & Moisture Barrier Thermally degrades at PE melting temps; discolors melt stream.
Inner Layer (PE) Heat-Sealing Capability Incompatible polymer phase separation; causes structural cracking in recyclate.

Resulting Engineering Breakdown

Without specialized chemical delamination or pure source separation, multi-layer films destroy the economic value of mechanical recyclate streams.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"Food packaging is a marvel of materials science: 5 distinct polymer layers bonded together in a sheet thinner than a human hair. But to a legacy collection scanner, it's just 'plastic'. When melted down, PE and PET don't mix—they behave like oil and water, leaving brittle inclusions."

SLIDE 08 / 16 · ROOT CAUSE DIAGNOSIS

Why Recycling Projects Fail: Failure Mode Analysis

08 / 16
FAILURE MODE 1

Feedstock Starvation

Plants built for 50,000 tons/year operate at 20% capacity because municipal collection streams are tied up in exclusive legacy contracts with rival disposal hubs.

FAILURE MODE 2

Catalyst Poisoning

Trace levels of halogenated flame retardants and PVC (>0.5%) in mixed municipal collection permanently poison chemical depolymerization catalysts.

FAILURE MODE 3

Cost-Margin Collapse

The energy required to clean and sort contaminated post-consumer collection exceeds the market price of virgin polymer when crude oil prices fall.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"Notice that none of these three failure modes are chemical or mechanical reactor failures. They are all collection, legal, and feedstock pricing failures. You can be the best chemical engineer in the world, but if your feedstock drops by 50% or contains 1% PVC, your plant goes bankrupt."

SLIDE 09 / 16 · LOGISTICS CONSTRAINTS

The Planning Fallacy in Logistics and Waste

09 / 16

Material balance equations on paper do not guarantee physical material movement in existing urban transportation networks.

Spatial & Structural Limits

Urban Density Constraints

Existing waste transfer stations in dense cities have zero physical footprint available for additional sorting optical lines or washing bays.

Axle-Load & Truck-Volume Limits

Transport Economics

Uncompacted plastic waste is extremely low density (~30 kg/m³). Transporting uncompacted clean plastic over 100 km burns more diesel energy than the plastic saves in virgin manufacturing.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"In logistics, we call this 'shipping air'. Plastic bottles and film are light but bulky. If you don't compact them, you are paying a truck driver to transport air across Germany. But if you DO compact them, you crush contaminants into the plastic. That is the brownfield paradox."

SLIDE 10 / 16 · SYSTEM REALISM

Systemic Boundaries: System Realism Index (SRI)

10 / 16

To evaluate whether a circular engineering design is grounded in brownfield reality or greenfield illusion, students calculate the System Realism Index (SRI):

QUANTITATIVE SYSTEM REALISM METRIC
SRI = 1 − ∑ Ignored External Constraints ∑ Total System Boundary Variables
Where Constraints include existing waste contracts, physical space limits, feedstock impurity variances, and transport emissions.
SRI Score Range System Classification Engineering Action
0.80 – 1.00 Brownfield-Validated System Ready for industrial pilot deployment.
0.40 – 0.79 Vulnerable Prototype Must expand system boundaries to incorporate collection constraints.
< 0.40 Academic Greenfield Trap Reject proposal; model fails outside laboratory conditions.
PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"From now on in this course, whenever you present a recycling design, you must present its SRI score. If you assume a pristine 99% pure input stream without explaining which existing collection infrastructure delivers it, your SRI is zero."

SLIDE 11 / 16 · METHODOLOGY SHIFT

Redesigning the Engineer’s Problem-Solving Approach

11 / 16

Old Mindset (The Greenfield Assumption)

"I will design an optimal chemical reactor or sorting process, and the surrounding logistics world will adapt to supply me with clean feedstock."

New Mindset (Brownfield Realism)

"What is currently in the yellow bin? What sorting contracts exist today? What contamination level must my process survive from day one?"

Rule of Engineering Robustness

Engineers must design for the worst-case legacy feedstock stream, not the best-case laboratory sample.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"I want you to invert your design process. Do not start at the factory gate. Start at the municipal household bin. Map the material journey backwards through the existing trucks and sorting plants. Your plant is merely an extension of that legacy pipeline."

SLIDE 16 / 16 · DESIGN FRAMEWORK

Design Paradigm: Design for vs. Design from Recycling

16 / 16

Design for Recycling (End-of-Life Focus)

Engineering products so they can be easily processed within existing recovery infrastructure at the end of their lifecycle.

Design from Recycling (The New Approach)

The Paradigm Shift: Intentionally engineering new products based on the actual, imperfect material properties of recyclates rather than ideal virgin polymers.

Concluding Design Takeaway

Design outward from the recyclate. Only when product design embraces secondary material tolerances do we truly close the brownfield loop.

PROFESSOR'S LECTURE NOTES

"The critical parting thought of this lecture series: Move beyond simply 'Design for Recycling'. We must embrace 'Design from Recycling'. Use the actual stochastic properties of secondary raw materials as your foundational design parameters."
